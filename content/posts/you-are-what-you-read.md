# You Are what you READ
I was recently perusing the Kindle store looking for books and I noticed the Saint of Steel series by [T. Kingfisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_Vernon) was deeply discounted. I had heard the Author's name in passing on a few occasions and heard good things so I figured would give the series a shot. I went in sight unseen other than I saw the word `paladin` in the description and went "sure".  

First thing out of the gate, I appreciated the subtle hints at worldbuilding, at first it seemed like pretty standard fantasy-fare. The character writing was witty and I enjoyed the characterization of the broken but believable characters. The people felt real. I got a few chapters in when some the banter started to take a turn. I was expecting fantasy, which the novel was delivering, but I was beginning to suspect that I was reading a romance novel.

## Things We're All too Young to Know

Now, I am not particularly picky about what books I read. I, in-fact, try to read a lot of things. Non-fiction seems to be about the only thing I don't reach for when reading. Most non-fiction books I have finished left an indelible mark on [me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fiery_Trial) for sure, but it really has to be a certain type of non-fiction to get me to stick with it. I, when left to my own devices, gravitate towards character-strong fantasy and science fiction novels. I don't particularly care for the world's most interesting, grounded, magic system or the most realistic space travel modality; but I do care about interesting characters and how they navigate their lives.

My exposure to romance was, as a late 90's to early 00's kid, finding [Fabio](https://www.goodreads.com/list/show/29286.The_One_Only_Fabio) adjacent novels at the local [Borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_%28retailer%29). Borders, for those unaware, was a big deal in small-town America as a child. It had carpetted floors, a dedicated kids section, a cafe built in, and an elevator. Let that soak in, an elevator: one of the most captivating modes of travel, second only to an escalator. At Borders, there would be books as far as the eye could see. We'd scamper through the store, like the shitty goblins we were, and lock in on the [real good stuff](https://sandrabrown.net/booklist/classic-romance/). We'd cackle at the judicious use of the word *throbbing* and delight in the discovery of some profane secret. We were on the *know*, all the other kids thought books were what you read in school, who even thought that they could also be smutty? The skin magazines hidden on the Borders magazine rack, right behind [Thrasher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrasher_(magazine)) and [Johnathan Davis's face on Kerrang!](https://www.kerrang.com/tags/jonathan-davis) were appealling to luggards in comparison to the fallic prose in this *forbidden literature*.  

Now, there is nothing particularly scandalous about enjoying such novels. Books are books; a respite from the world around us. An opportunity to walk in shoes of others and experience the world through perspectives which would otherwise never be our own. People should read what they enjoy and just enjoy it. The only wider consideration being that each of those experiences, no matter how small, accumulate on you. We are humans, we are malleable socially and, as result, learn, adapt, respond, and compartmentalize in response to the experience of others. Each tragedy, victory, trist, and lifelong romance in a novel becomes, in some small way, your own. So, here I am, a couple decades removed from Fabio, experiencing the deeply human tale of some fallen paladins, and I encounter the word *throbbing*.

## Love in the Time of Doomscrolling

We, as humans on the internet in 2026, are beset on [all](https://www.health.harvard.edu/mind-and-mood/doomscrolling-dangers) sides by ravenous, attention vampires. For awhile, it seemded the final, most desirable, object worth a trade was our collective [attention-spans](https://ojs.stanford.edu/ojs/index.php/intersect/article/view/3463). The never ending stream of conciousnous, when discretized into fifteen second chunks, allows for maximal [targeted advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeted_advertising) and, more usefully, leaves the average person [receptive](https://www.parents.com/what-it-means-if-your-teen-is-redpilled-12014168) for 21st century [propaganda](https://www.disinfo.eu/outreach/our-webinars/23-may-tiktok-in-the-era-of-propaganda-and-disinformation/). However, Artificial Intelligence&trade;, has come for the final, most guarded garden, [our very thoughts](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11023-025-09757-6). I have, anecdotally, had a couple different conversations with people who made heavy use of AI for things more than just raw productivity and they remarked how they felt they had lost their inner voice. The flattening of self was happening and they themselves were mixed as to whether it was everything they were promised. The final, most intractable, solution is the one in which we are dragged back into [the cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegory_of_the_cave) and clap in endless enjoyment as the shadows dance before us.
